    Complex visit scheduling, turned into a next step you can decide on.

    At a U.S. home healthcare SaaS, scheduling tangled in eligibility, visit-frequency, and staffing rules became bookable dates, clear status, and AI recommendations people can check and adjust.

    Overview

    About the project

    Context and challenge
    An AI scheduling platform that U.S. home healthcare operations teams use to coordinate clinician visits. Schedulers had to weigh patient eligibility, visit frequency, and staff availability together, which led to repeated questions and confusion.
    Rachel’s role
    • Joined as the founding designer (Senior Product Designer).
    • Defined the product design vision and strategy for the core AI scheduling platform with the CEO, translating business goals into end-to-end design direction from 0 to 1.
    • Built the company’s first Figma design system with the CTO and 6 front-end engineers.
    Collaboration (public scope)
    Worked with the CEO, CTO, and front-end engineers, and established the company’s first end-to-end design process shared by design, product, and engineering.
    Project type
    Previous-role project · U.S. home healthcare startup · 2025–2026

    A project Rachel Cho delivered in a previous role. It covers only what can be shared publicly; screens and data are new examples created for illustration.

    Problem

    Schedulers had to reconcile overlapping rules in their heads, so it was hard to tell right away which dates a visit could be booked on.

    Solution

    Bookable dates and status come first, reasons for limits are written as sentences, and AI recommendations appear as a next step people can confirm or adjust.

    Key decisions

    Key design decisions

    01

    Bookable dates before rules.

    Because overlapping eligibility, visit-frequency, and availability rules caused repeated questions and confusion, the design shows bookable dates and status first instead of explaining the rules.

    What changed for users Without memorizing rules, people can see on the calendar which dates they can choose now.

    3. Next step The AI-recommended date is shown, and the user confirms it or adjusts to another date before booking.

    02

    Plain sentences instead of internal codes.

    Internal codes were replaced with wording users could understand. Choosing a blocked date explains what is blocking it and what to do next.

    What changed for users People understand why a date is blocked without having to ask someone.

    03

    AI recommendations people can check and adjust.

    To improve both decision speed and confidence, defined how AI-generated schedule recommendations are surfaced, validated, and adjusted.

    What changed for users A person makes the call: accept the recommendation or switch to another time.

    What I learned

    In rule-heavy work, showing the choices available now helps people decide more than explaining the rules first. I also made it a design principle that AI recommendations turn into real decisions only when people can check and adjust them.

    Figures were measured and reported in the project and reflect several improvements applied together, not the result of a single screen or person.
