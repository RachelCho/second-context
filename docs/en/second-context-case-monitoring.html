    Among scattered signals, the issue to handle first comes first.

    On a global pharmaceutical company’s operations monitoring platform, information scattered across 8 dashboards was rebuilt around severity and business impact, with criteria for showing AI insights by investigation priority.

    • 30 min → to 3 minIncident investigation time reported in the project
    • 80% fasterIncident triage time
    • 40% fasterTicket processing time in developers’ internal tools

    Overview

    About the project

    Context and challenge
    An enterprise monitoring platform supporting vaccine operations, used by the company’s engineering teams. To investigate an incident, operators had to move between 8 dashboards to work out severity and impact.
    Rachel’s role
    • Joined as a contract Senior Product Designer through a UX design agency.
    • Conducted 15 stakeholder interviews to define how AI insights such as risk predictions, alerts, and confidence levels are surfaced and prioritized.
    Collaboration (public scope)
    Designed a generative-AI incident management system with a global IT company’s AI/ML teams, translating model outputs into actionable signals for enterprise users.
    Project type
    Previous-role project · Contract through an agency · 2024

    A project Rachel Cho delivered in a previous role. It covers only what can be shared publicly; screens and data are new examples created for illustration.

    Problem

    Investigating an incident meant moving across 8 dashboards and matching severity and impact by hand, so deciding where to start took time.

    Solution

    The information architecture was rebuilt around severity and downstream impact, and AI insights are shown by investigation priority instead of all at the same weight.

    Key decisions

    Key design decisions

    01

    One flow around severity and impact, instead of 8 dashboards.

    Because operators had to piece together severity and impact across 8 dashboards themselves, the information architecture was redesigned around severity and downstream impact.

    What changed for users People decide what to investigate first from one list, without jumping between screens.

    White-label screens recreated from the original project’s structure · Sample data

    02

    Put the issue to handle first, first.

    Technical signals (anomaly scores) and business impact (blocked teams and owners) are read together to put the item to handle first at the top. Selecting an item shows affected teams, related signals, owners, and history in one place.

    What changed for users Even with many alerts, investigation starts from the single most important item.

    1. Many alerts When alerts are listed in the order received, all with equal weight, responders have to work out for themselves what to check first.

    2. Priority to the top Even with a low anomaly score, an incident becomes P1 when several teams are blocked and no one owns it. This movement only demonstrates the principle; the real screen opens in this order from the start.

    3. Click to investigate Selecting an item shows affected teams, related signals, owner, and records in one place. Select another item to compare.

    03

    AI insights aren’t listed at equal weight.

    Rather than listing risk predictions, alerts, and confidence levels with equal weight, set criteria for showing them by investigation priority. Predictions are framed only as possibilities, with the related records behind them available to expand.

    What changed for users People can check the evidence right away before deciding whether to trust an AI summary.

    1. Gather related records Past operations data, related tickets, and incident history connected for the project are brought together.

    2. Find the pattern What the records have in common is found, and sequences that recurred in the same order are marked.

    3. Forecast summary The AI summary above the records shows the result first. It separates the observed pattern from the forecast, and “View related records” shows the evidence.

    Results and lessons

    Measured and reported results

    • Work outcome30 min → to 3 minIncident investigation time reported in the project

    Research scope

    • Research scope15Stakeholder interviews to define how AI insights are shown and prioritized

    What I learned

    On signal-heavy screens, the starting point was agreeing with the team on how to order an investigation, not showing every piece of information. AI output also becomes an actionable signal when it appears within those criteria, together with its evidence.

    Figures were measured and reported in the project; methods differ by metric. The demos are recreated for illustration, and the figures are not the result of any single demo.
